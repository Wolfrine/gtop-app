# How appeal claims are grounded

The useful question is which visible relationships may make a particular experience appealing to its intended audience, and whether those relationships survive real use. This study cannot define beauty for everyone.

## Evidence levels

| Level | What we can say | What we cannot infer |
|---|---|---|
| Rendered observation | Type size contrasts, alignment, controlled palette, visible state changes, motion sequence | The viewer's feelings or comprehension |
| Evaluator interpretation | A composition seems calm, energetic, playful or precise, with a stated visual reason | Measured human agreement |
| Creator statement | The designer describes intended mood or teaching purpose | That the intended effect occurred |
| Expert recognition | An identified jury recognized the submitted work | Universal appeal or suitability for GTOP |
| Human response | A named study or actual participant describes a preference under known conditions | Transfer to another audience, task or design without testing |

Reinecke and Gajos's CHI 2014 study collected 2.4 million website-appeal ratings from nearly 40,000 participants and found that preferences for colourfulness and visual complexity varied. This supports treating audience preference as a variable; it is not a recipe for GTOP's palette or a current-trend survey. [Authors' research page](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2014/reinecke14visual.shtml).

## What the later direction review should ask

Use comparable content and viewport sizes. Present alternatives before showing award labels. Ask the user, and representative learners if available, what they notice first, what the screen invites them to do, which details create confidence or curiosity, and what becomes tiring. Separate initial attraction, willingness to explore, and willingness to return for everyday work. Preserve disagreements instead of averaging them into false certainty. GTOP student responses remain an open validation item, not fabricated evidence.

## Motion and comfort

Observe movement under normal and reduced-motion preferences, then inspect whether meaning and actions remain available. W3C's explanation of WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 (AAA) distinguishes essential motion from nonessential interaction-triggered animation that users should be able to disable. Automatically initiated motion also has separate considerations. A preference being enabled in a capture does not prove that the site respects it; the rendered outcome must be reviewed. This stage is not a formal conformance audit. [W3C explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).
