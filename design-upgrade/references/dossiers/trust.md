# The Evolution of Trust

[Live source](https://ncase.me/trust/) · Inspected 9 October 2026 · Guided explanatory game

**Aesthetic description:** Playful, approachable, consequential. This is evaluator interpretation, not a measured human response.

## First impression

Hand-drawn characters and lettering establish a coherent playful world. In the actual game, a short prompt, two choices and the consequence diagram share a stable central stage.

## Aesthetic profile

| Aspect | Observed treatment |
|---|---|
| Composition | The opening has a single Play action. The inspected lesson concentrates the diagram between an explanation and two large answer choices. |
| Typography | Handwritten text matches the illustrations. Emphasis appears through weight and placement rather than many competing card styles. |
| Colour and material | Mostly black-and-white drawing makes red/blue player markers and yellow outcome highlights stand out. |
| Rhythm and density | The learner acts before the next explanation appears. The same scene is reused as the explanation becomes more specific. |
| Motion | A choice changes the explanatory text and highlights numerical outcomes in the diagram. This is evidence of a state transition with meaning, not merely decorative movement. |
| Identity | Characters, buttons, cursor emphasis, type and drawing strokes share the same visual language. |

## What makes it useful

The feedback explains why a choice has a consequence and updates the illustration in place. The learner does not have to mentally connect a distant notification with the changed state.

## Behavior actually inspected

Play → introductory explanation → play-a-game action → Cooperate. The resulting explanation stated the -1/+3 consequence and highlighted it alongside the 0/0 alternative. Before/after screenshots were visually verified.

## Limits and what not to transfer

The inspected portion covers entry and the first choice, not the full approximately 30-minute game. The mobile entry scales the stage down substantially, leaving small footer controls; this is a poor responsive pattern to copy. Handwriting is a stylistic reference, not a readability prescription for long GTOP questions.

## GTOP application hypothesis

For practice feedback, connect the selected response, correct reasoning and outcome in one stable area. For labs, let a prediction or manipulation reveal its consequence. Keep explicit text labels and accessible controls; the hand-drawn skin is optional.
